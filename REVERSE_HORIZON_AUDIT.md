# Reverse derivation: what the gravity coefficient demands of the horizon

## Result

Running the proposed mechanism from the gravitational coefficient backward
does more than repeat the forward chain. It exposes four exact requirements:

1. the screening factor fixes the **magnitude** of a normalized two-channel
   overlap to `lambda4`;
2. a KMS realization fixes the dimensionless modular gap to `log Q`;
3. geometric wedge normalization converts that gap to the boost frequency
   `log Q/(2*pi)`;
4. exact physical transport of the response is equivalent, at the full-
   algebra level, to a vacuum-preserving conditional expectation onto the pQ
   subalgebra.

The fourth item is the most useful new formulation. It replaces an unspecified
“horizon map” with a standard operator-algebraic object having an exact theorem
and a clean failure condition.

## 1. Start from the gravitational screening factor

Suppose a two-mode quadratic response has the general symmetric block

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\ b&c\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a\ne0,\quad c\ne0.
\]

Eliminating the second mode gives `a-b^2/c`. Relative to the original first
coefficient, the surviving fraction is

\[
S=\frac{a-b^2/c}{a}=1-\frac{b^2}{ac}.
\]

Demanding the PDT value

\[
S=1-\lambda_4^2
\]

is therefore equivalent to

\[
\boxed{b^2=\lambda_4^2ac.}
\]

For a positive block this says that the magnitude of the canonically
normalized overlap is exactly `lambda4`. The theorem
`relativeSchur_eq_screening_iff` proves this equivalence in Lean.

The reverse direction reveals one ambiguity that the final screening number
cannot remove: it fixes `|b|/sqrt(ac)`, but not the sign of `b` or a complex
phase. A causal or chiral convention must select that orientation. The positive
KMS defect supplies a natural candidate, but the gravitational scalar alone
does not.

## 2. Reverse the KMS response

For inverse temperature `beta` and positive spectral frequency `omega`, the
normalized ordered response is

\[
D(\beta,\omega)=1-e^{-\beta\omega}.
\]

Requiring `D=lambda4=1-1/Q` gives the exact equivalence

\[
\boxed{\beta\omega=\log Q.}
\]

The response fixes the product, rather than temperature and frequency
separately. The graph presentation uses

\[
\beta_Q=\log Q,\qquad \omega_{\rm gauge}=1.
\]

For a Rindler wedge, the conventional geometric normalization has inverse
temperature `2*pi` with respect to the boost generator. The same response then
requires

\[
\boxed{\omega_Q^{\rm boost}=\frac{\log Q}{2\pi}}
=0.0317451369793420\ldots .
\]

This is the precise reverse target for a geometric realization: locate a wedge
mode with that dimensionless boost charge. The Lean theorems
`kms_response_eq_quartic_iff` and `wedge_response_eq_quartic_iff` check both
equivalences.

This also clarifies the proposed tick. `log Q` is a dimensionless modular
action per quartic gauge step. It becomes a duration or an energy only after a
physical scale and a convention for the modular/boost parameter are supplied.

## 3. Why both p and Q appear when the argument is reversed

The quartic graph factor by itself has discrete modular ratios generated by
`Q^Z`; it is type `III_(1/Q)`. A generic relativistic wedge factor is
hyperfinite type `III_1`, whose nonzero Connes spectrum fills the positive
reals.

The cubic graph contributes the ratios `rho^Z`. Because

\[
\frac{\log\rho}{\log Q}\notin\mathbb Q,
\]

the joint group

\[
\{\rho^mQ^n:m,n\in\mathbb Z\}
\]

is dense in the positive reals. The tensor product of the two graph factors is
therefore hyperfinite type `III_1`.

Read backward from the wedge, this gives an exact division of labor:

- the p sector supplies the incommensurate modular scales needed for the
  continuous type-`III_1` environment;
- the Q sector retains a distinguished discrete line whose causal-response
  defect is `lambda4`.

This is a substantive pQ compatibility result. It does not make the pair
unique from factor type alone; many incommensurate scale pairs can generate a
dense subgroup. Any uniqueness claim must come from the independent PDT
selection of the founding pair.

## 4. Reverse the horizon map: an expected-subfactor criterion

Let

\[
\pi:N_{pQ}\longrightarrow M_W
\]

be the state-preserving injection supplied abstractly by the type-III
embedding theorem, with

\[
\omega_W\circ\pi=\phi_{pQ}.
\]

State preservation fixes static expectation values, but it does not make the
range invariant under wedge modular flow. For the full modular dynamics, the
needed equation is

\[
\sigma_t^{\omega_W}(\pi(x))
=\pi(\sigma_t^{\phi_{pQ}}(x)).
\]

Takesaki's conditional-expectation theorem gives an equivalent target under
the standard faithful-normal hypotheses. The range `pi(N_pQ)` is globally
invariant under the wedge modular group if and only if there exists a normal
vacuum-preserving conditional expectation

\[
\boxed{
E:M_W\longrightarrow\pi(N_{pQ}),
\qquad E^2=E,
\qquad \omega_W\circ E=\omega_W.}
\]

If this `E` exists, the restricted wedge modular flow is exactly the modular
flow of the restricted state. Hence the quartic mode keeps its `log Q`
modular gap and its response `lambda4` automatically.

This is the information-geometric form of the missing bridge. The map `E` is a
noncommutative coarse-graining that forgets the rest of the wedge algebra while
preserving the pQ statistics exactly. In statistical language, the embedded
pQ algebra is a sufficient observable subalgebra for those response values.

The existing state-embedding theorem does not guarantee such an expectation.
This is a real strengthening, not a change of words.

There is no obstruction at the level of the bare hyperfinite factor: tensor
self-absorption constructs an expected pQ copy for a suitably chosen product
state. The unresolved intersection is preservation of the **specified wedge
vacuum**. `EXPECTED_SUBFACTOR_STATUS.md` separates these two existence results
and gives the first modular-eigenoperator test.

## 5. What the deposited action looks like backward

The reverse quadratic condition requires

\[
\frac{b^2}{ac}=\lambda_4^2.
\]

At the selected axis vacuum, the deposited action has `b=0`. Its tree-level
relative Schur coefficient is therefore `1`, not `1-lambda4^2`. The first
portal coupling is cubic, `2*kappa*v*h*x^2`, so it can alter the interacting
quartic response but does not force the required quadratic overlap.

This shows why further manipulation of the static potential is unlikely to
close the mechanism. The target is an invariant operator subalgebra or a
protected modular eigenline, not another identity among potential
coefficients.

## 6. The next proof target

The subsequent free-wedge calculation rules out the original bounded-local
version of this target in the linearized two-scalar vacuum: there is no
nonzero bounded boost eigenoperator at the required frequency. The
continuous-core calculation realizes the frequency and the quartic trace-ray
polynomial, but global trace normalization cancels from entropy variations.
The strongest clean completion is therefore now:

1. retain the generalized quartic clock in the continuous core or observer
   algebra;
2. derive the standing F373 affine Hodge operator
   `A=I+lambda4 C` as a normalized gravitational kinetic stiffness;
3. show that the physical source reads its orientation-even inverse, which is
   exactly `I/(1-lambda4^2)`;
4. recover the equivalent inverse-length amplitude
   `sqrt(1-lambda4^2)` and horizon area-density calibration;
5. apply Jacobson's area law to the same response.

Step 1 and the operator algebra in step 2 have standard or exact realizations.
Deriving that operator from the action and fixing the physical source in steps
2--3 is the remaining physical theorem.
See `FREE_WEDGE_CORE_AUDIT.md`, `TRACE_NORMALIZATION_OBSTRUCTION.md`, and
`HORIZON_AREA_CALIBRATION.md`.

The earlier expected-subfactor construction remains meaningful for interacting
or non-Fock models, but it is no longer the shortest route to the gravity
factor.

## Falsifiers

The reverse chain fails if any of the following occurs:

- the physical response block has normalized overlap different from
  `lambda4`;
- no exact wedge spectral line has modular gap `log Q`;
- every state-preserving pQ embedding fails modular invariance;
- the relative cocycle shifts or mixes the quartic line;
- no physical affine Hodge stiffness or equivalent noncentral response yields
  the orientation-even factor `1/(1-lambda4^2)`.
