# Three-dimensional presents as information packings

## The proposed physical picture

A spatial present is a compatibility problem. Information must coexist in one
three-dimensional configuration with definite separation, adjacency, shape,
and boundary. Time is then the ordered passage from one compatible packing to
the next, rather than an additional substance laid beside space.

In PDT language, the proposal is

\[
\rho\text{-sector}
\;\longleftrightarrow\;
\text{settled spatial packing and record},
\]

\[
Q\text{-sector}
\;\longleftrightarrow\;
\text{non-settling update between spatial packings}.
\]

The first line now has a striking direct theorem behind it. The second remains
a physical interpretation of an exact obstruction.

## A published three-dimensional plastic-number construction

Bandt and Meyer study finite-digit self-similarity equations

\[
\Lambda=\theta\Lambda+F
\]

for point patterns in Euclidean space. Their Proposition 7 is explicitly
three-dimensional. Let `rho` be the plastic number, and choose four points
`a,b,c,d` in `R^3` which are not coplanar and satisfy

\[
a+b+c+d=0.
\]

With

\[
H=\{0,a,b,c,d\},
\]

they prove that there is a Meyer set

\[
\Lambda_\rho
=\bigcup_{m\ge1}(H+\rho H+\cdots+\rho^mH)
\subset\mathbb R^3
\]

satisfying the plastic-number self-similarity equation.

This is stronger than saying that `rho` is compatible with quasicrystal
mathematics. It gives a concrete uniformly discrete, relatively dense
self-similar pattern in the physical ambient dimension `R^3`, generated by
the smallest Pisot number.

## The explicit tetrahedral witness

The Lean artifact uses

\[
\begin{aligned}
a&=(1,1,1),& b&=(1,-1,-1),\\
c&=(-1,1,-1),& d&=(-1,-1,1).
\end{aligned}
\]

These are the four vertices of a regular tetrahedron centered at the origin.
Lean proves

\[
a+b+c+d=0
\]

and that the determinant of the three difference vectors based at `a` is

\[
\det[b-a,c-a,d-a]=-16\ne0.
\]

They therefore satisfy exactly the noncoplanarity and balance hypotheses of
Bandt--Meyer Proposition 7. The local digit geometry is a center plus the
four vertices of the simplest three-dimensional simplex. Its boundary has
four triangular faces and is intrinsically two-dimensional.

This geometry does not by itself prove that physical space is the Meyer set.
It supplies an explicit, theorem-backed candidate for what “three-dimensional
information packing” can mean mathematically.

## The exact obstruction on the Q side

Bandt--Meyer Theorem 1 states that if a nontrivial solution of

\[
\Lambda=\theta\Lambda+F
\]

is uniformly discrete and `theta<2`, then `theta` must be a Pisot number.

The positive root `Q` of

\[
Q^4=Q+1
\]

lies between 1 and 2 and is not Pisot: its complex conjugate pair has modulus
greater than one. Therefore a nontrivial uniformly discrete solution with
inflation factor `Q` cannot exist. In particular, replacing `rho` by `Q` in
the same tetrahedral finite-sum construction cannot yield a uniformly
separated infinite point pattern.

The theorem is dimension-independent. It says more than “we have not found a
Q packing”: within this finite-digit self-similar class, a settled Q packing
is impossible.

## Coverage and separation split at the divide

The three-dimensional construction separates two requirements which are easy
to blur together.

First comes **coverage**. Let `K` be the tetrahedron spanned by the four
balanced digits and put `T=3K`. The centered-simplex identity is

\[
T+\{a,b,c,d\}=4K=\frac43T.
\]

Hence, whenever `theta<4/3`, iterating the inclusion of `theta*T` in `T+H`
shows that `T+Lambda` covers all of `R^3`. This is the relative-density half
of the Bandt--Meyer argument. Lean verifies the exact root locations

\[
\phi>\frac43,\qquad \rho<\frac43,\qquad Q<\frac43.
\]

Second comes **separation**. For a scale below 2, Bandt--Meyer Theorem 1 says
that uniform discreteness forces the scale to be Pisot. Thus `rho` has both
coverage and separation, whereas `Q` has coverage but cannot have separation.
The Q construction fills three-space while producing pairs of records that
become arbitrarily close.

The `phi` control now has a precise role. It is Pisot and therefore lies on the
separated side, but it is above the `4/3` sufficient threshold for this
balanced tetrahedral coverage argument. This does not prove that no
three-dimensional `phi` construction can exist; it proves that `phi` does not
pass this particular dimension-matched gateway.

## What this establishes, and what it does not

The mathematical divide is:

| Scale | `4/3` coverage gateway | separation status | Combined result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `phi` | Above the sufficient threshold | Pisot; finite control stays separated | Separated, but not selected by this 3D coverage proof |
| `rho` | Below threshold | Pisot | Explicit tetrahedral Meyer set in `R^3` |
| `Q` | Below threshold | Non-Pisot | Relatively dense but necessarily non-uniformly discrete |

The golden ratio `phi` is an essential control. The same finite tetrahedral
experiment also shows a stable minimum separation for `phi` through depth 8.
Consequently, stable separation distinguishes the Pisot side from `Q`; it
does not by itself prove that `rho`, rather than another Pisot scale, uniquely
encodes three dimensions.

There is a sharp classification behind this control:
Aarts--Fokkink--Kruijtzer prove that `phi` and `rho` are the only morphic
numbers, formalized in Palomar entry `PALOMAR-2026-09-01-000008`. In the
golden family they are the quadratic and cubic settled cases before the
quartic non-Pisot transition. Together with the threshold inequalities, this
makes `rho` the unique morphic number below the tetrahedral `4/3` coverage
gateway. Reading this construction as physical three-space remains the PDT
dimensional identification.

This supports a precise use of the words **settled** and **non-settling**.
Uniform discreteness supplies a positive minimum separation: distinct records
do not collapse into arbitrarily close alternatives. A Meyer difference set
also retains controlled arithmetic order. On the Q side, arbitrarily close
alternatives must eventually appear in this construction.

The following statements remain physical conjectures:

* a physical 3D present is represented by this or a related `rho`-Meyer
  packing;
* the failure of Q to settle is temporal evolution rather than merely a bad
  spatial encoding;
* successive Q-driven updates assemble into a four-dimensional causal
  history;
* gravitational curvature is the compatibility cost of passing between
  successive settled 3D packings.

The packing theorem makes these conjectures sharper and more testable; it does
not prove them by itself.

## Direct numerical test

Use the same tetrahedral digit set for both roots and form the nested finite
approximants

\[
\Lambda_m(\theta)=H+\theta H+\cdots+\theta^mH.
\]

For each depth, compute the minimum distance between distinct points and the
growth of the difference set.

* At `theta=rho`, the minimum distance must remain bounded away from zero in
  the infinite construction.
* At `theta=Q`, the minimum distance must approach zero along some sequence of
  depths; otherwise the limit would contradict Bandt--Meyer Theorem 1.
* At `theta=phi`, the same finite test controls against mistaking a general
  Pisot separation effect for a uniquely three-dimensional `rho` effect.

This is not needed to prove the published theorem. It provides a visual and
quantitative model of “settling” versus “continued repacking,” and may reveal
the rate at which Q alternatives crowd together. That rate is a candidate to
compare with the quartic clock and screening exponents.
