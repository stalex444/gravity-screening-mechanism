# From the quartic erasure code to the graviton polarizations

## The exact specialization

The finite quartic Stinespring dilation in this repository was constructed for
an arbitrary `n`-state internal data vector `psi`.  Its one-particle action is

\[
V_s\psi
=\sqrt{s}\,\psi_{\rm ext}\oplus
 \sqrt{1-s}\,\psi_{\rm hid}.
\]

The same data vector appears in both ports.  The channel decides which port is
accessible; it does not inspect or change the internal data label.

`GravityScreening/ErasureTTBridge.lean` now sets `n=2` and embeds the two real
transverse-traceless graviton amplitudes as

\[
\psi=(h_+,h_\times)\in\mathbb C^2.
\]

Reading the real amplitude in the exterior data port gives exactly

\[
(h_+,h_\times)_{\rm ext}
=\sqrt{s}\,(h_+,h_\times).
\]

In matrix form this is

\[
C_{\rm ext}=\sqrt{s}\,I_2.
\]

At the quartic weight,

\[
s=S_Q=1-\lambda_4^2=\frac{2Q-1}{Q^2},
\]

so the exterior port of the previously constructed information channel is
definitionally the TT block selected independently by the response-uniqueness
theorem.

## The covariance theorem

The Lean file also proves, for every finite complex matrix `U`,

\[
V_{s,{\rm ext}}(U\psi)=U V_{s,{\rm ext}}(\psi).
\]

This is stronger than covariance under one chosen polarization rotation.  The
retained channel commutes with every linear change of the internal data
coordinates because its amplitude is a scalar.  In particular, it commutes
with the plus/cross quarter-turn generated by a rotation in the transverse
plane.

The two independent arguments now meet:

1. the erasure dilation directly produces `sqrt(S_Q) I_2` when its data space
   has two labels;
2. rotational covariance, self-adjointness, weight `S_Q`, and the passive
   branch prove that `sqrt(S_Q) I_2` is the unique admissible minimal TT
   response.

Thus neither a polarization basis nor a mixing angle is being chosen to make
the gravity formula work.

## Direct composition with gravity

Perturbative gravity relates canonical and physical TT amplitudes by

\[
\sigma=\frac{\kappa}{2}h,
\qquad
\kappa^2=32\pi G.
\]

The new capstone theorem starts from the exterior coordinates read directly
from the erasure dilation.  For every nonzero TT polarization vector, it
proves

\[
\frac{\kappa_Q}{2}V_{Q,{\rm ext}}(h)
=\frac{\kappa_0}{2}h
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
G_Q=\frac{G_0}{S_Q}.
\]

No separately entered TT attenuation coefficient occurs in this theorem.
The coefficient comes from the quartic erasure map.

## The remaining physical identification

The Lean specialization does not establish that a physical horizon presents
the graviton polarization as the two-state data carried by the Q erasure
channel.  That statement is now the central physical seam.

A field-theoretic derivation should construct an encoding

\[
E:\mathcal H_{\rm TT}\longrightarrow\mathcal H_{Q,{\rm data}}
\]

with three properties:

1. `E` preserves the canonical graviton norm fixed by the horizon shear
   action;
2. `E` intertwines transverse rotations and modular evolution;
3. restriction of the physical horizon evolution to `E(H_TT)` is the quartic
   erasure dilation already proved here.

If such an encoding is derived from the local first-order prepotential action
or the sharp horizon algebra, the linear physical identification is complete:
the new capstone then supplies the Newton response, while the existing
Pauli--Fierz uniqueness and source-frame theorems propagate it to the static
electric sector.

A failure of norm preservation, an extra state-dependent attenuation, a
polarization-dependent exterior weight, or a modular-frequency shift would
refute this minimal identification.
